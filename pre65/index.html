Section 1

A client base hiding in plain sight

Early retirees are some of the best clients in the individual market. They're organized and financially literate, they plan years ahead, and they stay with an advisor they trust. Most agents never go after them.

3×

ACA age rating lets a 64-year-old be charged up to three times what a 21-year-old pays, so these households have the most premium at stake and the biggest possible tax credits.

1–10 yrs

That's how long they need coverage before Medicare, renewed every Open Enrollment, each year a new chance to review and retain.

Age 65

That's when they move to Medicare, and the agent who guided them through the gap is the obvious choice to write their Medicare business.

Why they need an advisor, not a quote

  • The enrollment rules aren't intuitive. Retiring doesn't create a Special Enrollment Period. Losing job-based coverage does.
  • Their income is a choice. Unlike a W-2 employee, a retiree decides how much income shows up on the tax return through withdrawals, conversions and asset sales. Those decisions set the subsidy.
  • The penalty for getting it wrong is large. The 400% FPL cliff and the end of repayment caps mean a single bad withdrawal can cost a household thousands in premium tax credits.
  • Their other advisors usually don't see the health insurance side. Financial planners and CPAs know the tax code but rarely know marketplace rules. The agent who connects the two becomes the one they rely on.

Section 2

Enrollment & Special Enrollment Period rules

Outside Open Enrollment, a consumer needs a qualifying life event. The most common mistake retirees make is assuming the retirement itself qualifies.

SituationSEP?What to tell the client
Retiring (by itself)NoRetirement isn't a qualifying event. The loss of coverage that comes with it is.
Losing job-based coverage at retirementYesLoss of minimum essential coverage opens a 60-day window, and they can apply up to 60 days before the coverage ends. Plan the date so there's no gap.
Spouse's employer coverage endsYesEach person losing coverage qualifies, including dependents.
Electing COBRA, then dropping it mid-yearNoVoluntarily ending COBRA or not paying premiums outside Open Enrollment isn't a qualifying event. They can switch at Open Enrollment.
COBRA runs out (exhaustion)YesReaching the end of the COBRA period counts as loss of coverage.
Retiree health plan ends or employer drops itYesInvoluntary loss of retiree coverage qualifies.
Moving to a new state or ZIP in retirementOftenA permanent move qualifies if they had qualifying coverage for at least one of the 60 days before the move.
Open EnrollmentAnyoneNo event needed. Right now is the cleanest time to move a retiree off COBRA.

COBRA and retiree coverage: eligible vs. enrolled

Being eligible for COBRA or retiree coverage doesn't block the premium tax credit. Being enrolled does, for every month they stay on it. Run the marketplace numbers before the client signs the COBRA election. COBRA often costs 102% of the full group premium, and a subsidized marketplace plan can come in far lower.

The retirement-year trap: eligibility for the premium tax credit is based on annual household income. A client who retires in June still reports the January–June salary, PTO payout, severance and bonus for that year. Projected income for the retirement year may push them over the cliff even though their January income will be much lower. Compare COBRA for the rest of the first year with a marketplace plan starting January 1, then pick the one that costs less.

Section 3

What counts as MAGI, dollar for dollar

Marketplace subsidies are based on household Modified Adjusted Gross Income: AGI, plus tax-exempt interest, plus the non-taxable portion of Social Security, plus excluded foreign income. Many retirees and even some advisors get this wrong.

Income sourceCounts?Notes
Traditional IRA / 401(k) / 403(b) withdrawalsYes, fullyEvery pre-tax dollar withdrawn is MAGI.
Roth conversionsYes, fullyThe converted amount is taxable income in the year of conversion. This is the most common reason retirees go over the cliff.
Social Security benefitsYes, 100%ACA MAGI adds back the non-taxable part, so the whole benefit counts, not just the taxable portion (up to 85%).
Pensions and annuity paymentsYesOnly the taxable portion counts. Return of after-tax basis doesn't.
Part-time, consulting or 1099 incomeYesCounted net of business expenses and the deductible half of SE tax.
Capital gains, dividends, interestYesTax-exempt muni interest counts too. Only the gain on a sale counts, not the principal.
Qualified Roth IRA / Roth 401(k) withdrawalsNoTax-free and not part of MAGI, a key tool for managing income.
Cash savings, return of principalNoSpending from a checking or savings account doesn't create income.
HSA qualified distributionsNoTax-free when used for qualified medical expenses.
Loan proceeds, reverse-mortgage draws, giftsNoNot income.

Household MAGI includes the tax filer, spouse, and any dependents required to file a return. Married couples generally must file jointly to claim the credit (limited exceptions apply).

Section 4

The 400% FPL subsidy cliff

The enhanced subsidies that removed the upper income cap expired at the end of 2025. For Plan Year 2027, households with income above 400% of the federal poverty level get no premium tax credit at all. Below the line, the expected contribution is capped at roughly 10% of income, so at age 60–64 the credit can easily be worth more than $15,000 a year for a couple.

Illustrative couple, age 62 and 63

Benchmark silver premium: about $2,100/month ($25,200/yr).

  • MAGI $86,000 (397% FPL): expected contribution of about 10% = ~$8,600, so the tax credit is about $16,600.
  • MAGI $87,000 (402% FPL): tax credit is $0.

An extra $1,000 IRA withdrawal costs this couple about $16,600. Figures are illustrative. Premiums vary by ZIP code and carrier.

No more safety net at tax time

Starting with tax year 2026, the caps that limited how much excess advance credit a household had to repay are gone. If a client's actual income goes over the cliff, they repay every dollar of the advance credit when they file.

That makes income projection the most valuable thing you do for these clients, and the reason to check in mid-year and again in Q4.

The floor matters too

Early retirees living on cash savings or Roth money can report income so low that it causes a different problem:

  • Below 138% FPL in a Medicaid-expansion state ($22,025 single / $29,863 couple): they're likely Medicaid-eligible instead of subsidy-eligible. MAGI Medicaid has no asset test, so a household with $2M in savings may still qualify. Many retirees don't want that. Plan income above the line on purpose.
  • Below 100% FPL in a non-expansion state ($15,960 single / $21,640 couple): no premium tax credit (the “coverage gap”). Realizing capital gains or doing a small Roth conversion can move them into the eligible range.

The target for most pre-65 retirees is a range, not a single number: enough income to stay above the floor, low enough to stay below 400%, and ideally low enough to qualify for cost-sharing reductions on Silver plans (at or below 250% FPL).

Section 6

Levers that pull clients back under the cliff

You aren't the tax preparer, but you should know which levers exist so you can bring them up and coordinate with the client's financial advisor or CPA.

1. Mix the withdrawal sources

Fund living expenses from a combination of cash, taxable brokerage principal, and Roth money so only part of each year's spending counts as MAGI. Only the gain on a taxable sale counts, not the whole sale.

2. Right-size Roth conversions

Convert up to the cliff, not past it. A conversion done in January can't be undone (recharacterization is gone), so many planners do conversions in Q4, once the rest of the year's income is known.

3. Time Social Security

Since 100% of the benefit counts, delaying Social Security until 65 or later often keeps MAGI lower during the pre-Medicare years and increases the lifetime benefit.

4. Use HSA contributions

An above-the-line deduction that, unlike a traditional IRA contribution, doesn't require earned income. See the next section.

5. Bunch big events

A home sale, a large one-time withdrawal, or a big conversion often belongs in a single “sacrifice” year, or after Medicare starts, instead of spread across every subsidy year.

6. Harvest gains on purpose

For clients near the floor, realizing capital gains can raise MAGI into the eligible range while resetting cost basis, so it helps on both fronts.

Your talking point: “Your financial advisor plans how much income you take each year. I make sure those numbers keep your health insurance subsidy. Let's get all three of us on the same page before you take your next withdrawal.”

Section 7

The HSA play

Pairing an HSA-qualified plan with HSA contributions is one of the few ways a retiree can lower MAGI after the income is already earned.

  1. Enroll in an HSA-qualified plan. Starting in 2026, marketplace Bronze and Catastrophic plans count as HSA-compatible under federal law, along with any plan the carrier designates as an HSA-qualified HDHP. That gives you many more options than before.
  2. Open an HSA. Any HSA custodian works. It doesn't have to be tied to an employer. A spouse 55+ who wants the catch-up needs their own HSA.
  3. Contribute up to the 2027 limit. $4,500 self-only / $9,000 family, plus $1,000 for each eligible person 55 or older. A couple 55+ on family coverage can deduct up to $11,000.
  4. Lower MAGI dollar for dollar. The contribution is an above-the-line deduction, so no earned income is needed, unlike a traditional IRA.
  5. Make the final decision after year-end. 2027 contributions can be made until the 2027 tax filing deadline (April 2028). If the client's final numbers come in slightly over the cliff, a contribution may bring them back under before they file.

HSA guardrails to review with the client:

  • Contribution limits are prorated for months not covered by an HSA-qualified plan (with a “last-month rule” exception and a testing period).
  • Contributions must stop once the client enrolls in Medicare. Starting Social Security at or after 65 automatically enrolls them in Part A, and late Part A enrollment can be backdated up to 6 months, so plan the last contribution carefully.
  • Funds already in the HSA can be used tax-free for qualified expenses for life, including Medicare premiums (not Medigap) after 65.
  • Compare total cost: a lower premium and the tax savings against a higher deductible. A Silver plan with cost-sharing reductions may still be the better fit for lower-income households.

Section 8

The Medicare handoff: a client for life

The pre-65 relationship has a natural next step. Map it out from the first meeting so the client knows you'll be with them at 65.

WhenWhat you do
Age 63Bring up IRMAA. Medicare Part B and D premiums at 65 are based on MAGI from two years earlier, so large conversions at 63 can raise Medicare premiums at 65.
3 months before 65Initial Enrollment Period opens. Enroll in Parts A & B. Review Medigap vs. Medicare Advantage and Part D.
Medicare start monthOnce eligible for premium-free Part A, the premium tax credit ends. Cancel the marketplace plan for the Medicare-eligible spouse, because the marketplace doesn't end it automatically. Repaying credits for overlap months is a common, avoidable mistake.
Younger spouseStays on the marketplace plan, re-run the application with the new household coverage, and keep managing MAGI until their own 65th birthday. That's often several more years of service.

Also look at your existing Medicare book. Medicare clients with a spouse under 65 who's retiring are pre-65 prospects you already have a relationship with.

The blueprint: find, close and keep

Where to find pre-65 retirees

Referral partners

  • Fee-only financial planners and RIAs
  • CPAs and enrolled agents (especially Q4 tax planning)
  • Estate-planning attorneys
  • Retirement-income and annuity specialists

Your own book

  • Younger spouses of Medicare clients
  • Individual clients turning 55–64
  • Life, annuity and P&C clients near retirement
  • Past COBRA quotes that never converted

Life events

  • Early-retirement and voluntary separation packages
  • Layoffs and plant closings (public WARN notices)
  • Retiring teachers, public-safety and municipal employees whose retiree coverage is limited or ends
  • Business owners selling a company

Content & community

  • Short videos on the cliff and SEP myths
  • “Retiring before 65?” workshops at libraries and community centers
  • LinkedIn posts aimed at people 55–64 and their advisors
  • Co-hosted webinars with a financial planner

The discovery conversation

  1. When does your employer coverage end, and is there a retiree plan or COBRA offer?
  2. Who's in your tax household, and when does each person turn 65?
  3. What income will you have this year and next year: pension, Social Security, withdrawals, consulting, investment income?
  4. Is a Roth conversion, home sale or large withdrawal planned in the next few years?
  5. Which doctors, hospitals and prescriptions must stay in network?
  6. Who is your financial advisor or CPA, and may I coordinate with them?

A talk track that positions you as the advisor

“Most people don't realize that retiring doesn't give you a special enrollment period, but losing your work coverage does. And because your subsidy is based on your retirement income, a single large IRA withdrawal or Roth conversion can cost you thousands. My job is to set up your coverage so the income plan you've already built doesn't cost you your tax credit, and then guide you into Medicare when you turn 65.”

Section 10

Compliance guardrails

  • Complete Plan Year 2027 Marketplace registration and training before you enroll anyone. See the ACA Certification Hub for federal and state requirements.
  • Document consumer consent and review the eligibility application with the client before submission, as federal Marketplace rules require.
  • Use the client's own good-faith income projection. You can explain what counts as income, but the client attests to the number.
  • Stay in your lane on tax and investment advice. Explain how the rules work, and send specific tax, withdrawal and conversion decisions to a CPA, enrolled agent or licensed financial professional.
  • Follow your state's insurance department rules and each carrier's marketing guidelines for ads, social posts and seminars.

Section 11

Frequently asked questions

Does a big 401(k) balance disqualify someone from subsidies?

No. Marketplace subsidies look at income, not assets. A household with a large balance can still qualify if the amount they withdraw keeps MAGI under 400% FPL.

Does income from the year before retirement matter?

The Marketplace uses projected income for the coverage year. The retirement year itself includes any wages earned before retiring, which is why the first partial year often needs separate planning.

What if a client's income comes in higher than projected?

They reconcile on Form 8962. Starting with tax year 2026, there's no cap on repaying excess advance credits. Clients near the cliff should report income changes during the year, and may be able to use a deductible HSA contribution before filing.

Can a client take less advance credit to be safe?

Yes. Clients can choose to take only part of the credit in advance and claim the rest at tax time. That can be a sensible cushion for retirees whose income is uncertain.

Are tax-free Roth withdrawals really excluded?

Qualified Roth distributions aren't included in AGI and aren't added back for ACA MAGI. Non-qualified distributions of earnings can be taxable, so confirm the client's Roth holding period and age with their tax advisor.

Are the enhanced subsidies coming back?

As of this guide's review date, the enhanced credits have expired and the 400% FPL limit is back for Plan Year 2027. Congress could change that. Check current law before quoting, and update the calculator's contribution percentage when the IRS publishes the 2027 figure.
